About the practical context

A rapid review is conducted with practitioners, bounded to a practical problem and inside their context; it cuts cost and time on purpose (days or weeks) and reports what it found in a medium practitioners read.

Demand. Practitioners may ask for it; a researcher may align their research agenda with practitioners' needs, or prospect a practical problem and offer the review. Practical problem. Defined with the practitioners, for instance in interviews or a focus group. Stakeholders. Who takes part and how much: practitioners usually have no account here, so write their roles. Time frame. When it started and when the answer is needed.

Every concession made to be rapid goes into the protocol: see the Rapid Review Checklist.

B. Cartaxo, G. Pinto and S. Soares, "Rapid Reviews in Software Engineering", arXiv:2003.10006, 2020, §4.1.
