About the threats to validity

A review reports what may limit the validity of its results, and what was done to reduce it: the strengths and weaknesses of the review, in the discussion of the report (Kitchenham & Charters, 2007, §7).

Petersen et al. (2015, §3.6) discuss them by type, after Petersen and Gencel (2013): descriptive validity (are the observations described accurately?), theoretical validity (did the review capture what it meant to, given the search, the sample and the biases of the researchers?), generalizability (within the population and beyond it), interpretive validity (are the conclusions reasonable given the data?) and repeatability. Many reviews use the types of Wohlin et al. (2012): construct, internal, external and conclusion validity.

None of these is required here: give each threat the type that fits your review, or none.

B. Kitchenham and S. Charters, "Guidelines for performing Systematic Literature Reviews in Software Engineering", EBSE Technical Report, 2007. K. Petersen, S. Vakkalanka and L. Kuzniarz, "Guidelines for conducting systematic mapping studies in software engineering: An update", Information and Software Technology, 64, 2015. C. Wohlin et al., "Experimentation in Software Engineering", Springer, 2012.
